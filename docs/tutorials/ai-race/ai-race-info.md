# Ready, Set, Race! 

![Banner](/static/tutorials/ai-race/racebanner.png "AI race")

**Educator Guide**

The **[Ready, Set, Race!](https://aka.ms/race)** tutorial introduces students to Artificial Intelligence (AI) through a fun game-based learning approach.

Students will train an AI bot to drive a race car based on data from their own driving patterns. They can then compete against the AI bot in a race!

## Lesson overview

**For:** students between the ages of 8 and 15 years old

**Time:** 45 minutes including game play

**Experience:** No previous coding background required

**Technology Requirements:**
- A desktop, laptop, tablet, or Chromebook with internet access
- Chrome or Edge browsers preferred
- Access to the [MakeCode Arcade](https://arcade.makecode.com) website.  Test domains here: https://arcade.makecode.com/domains

Note: this tutorial does not use generative AI and students will not be exposed to an AI chatbot through this experience.

**Learning Outcomes:**
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
- Understand what Artificial Intelligence refers to
- Explain imitation learning as a type of machine learning in which a model learns from examples or demonstrations
- Describe the role of training data in an AI system, recognizing that their steering, acceleration, braking, and other driving actions become examples the AI learns from
- Interpret a visualization of training data, using the Memories chart to identify patterns
- Make predictions about AI behavior based on its training data
- Train and test an AI model through an iterative process, observing its behavior and collecting additional training data to improve performance
- Evaluate how the quality and variety of training examples affect AI performance
- Investigate AI generalization by testing how a model trained through one set of experiences performs in different racing environments or on different tracks

**Key vocabulary:**
- **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** - the ability of a computer or robot to appear to think and act like a human
- **Imitation Learning** - a machine learning approach where AI agents acquire skills by mimicking expert behavior and demonstrations. This approach is widely used in robotics, autonomous driving, and control tasks.
- **Training data** - information used to teach machine learning models how to recognize patterns, make predictions, or perform tasks
- **Prediction** - the ability of a computer to anticipate future events or trends by learning patterns from past data

**CSTA Standards Alignment:**
- E4-ALG-ML-02: Analyze relationships between the properties of training data and a machine learning model's output.
- E3-ALG-ML-02: Investigate how a machine learning model can change when new data is added to a training set.
- E5-ALG-ML-02: Train a machine learning model to make a classification or prediction.
- MS-ALG-PS-05: Use an AI tool to generate outputs that assist in solving a computational problem
- MS-ALG-ML-08: Evaluate the features and limitations of a machine learning model.


**Suggested curriculum tie-in:**
- Computer Science & AI Literacy
- Data Science and Math
- Game Design
- Robotics

## Tutorial Steps

1. **Learn to drive** - students will gain familiarity with the driving controls by following a short tutorial <br/>
![Learn to Drive](/static/tutorials/ai-race/learntodrive.png "Learn to Drive")

2. **Train an AI** - students will drive around the race track collecting data points that will be used to train an AI bot <br/>
![Train AI](/static/tutorials/ai-race/teachai.png "Train AI")

3. **View data** - students will analyze the data in the Memories chart <br/>
![View data](/static/tutorials/ai-race/viewdata.png "View data")

4. **Test AI** - students will watch as their AI drives the car around the track using the data it was trained on. They have the option to go back and train again if they are not happy with their AI bot's performance. <br/>
![Test AI](/static/tutorials/ai-race/testai.png "Test AI")

5. **Race setup** - students can customize their car's logo design and add background music to their race <br/>
![Setup](/static/tutorials/ai-race/songlogo.png "Setup race")

6. **Race** - students can choose a racetrack and race against their AI bots! <br/>
![Race](/static/tutorials/ai-race/race.png "Race")

## Classroom Discussion Questions

### Training data
- Which of your driving actions became training examples for your AI bot? 
- What might happen if you only demonstrated acceleration and never braking?
- What patterns do you notice in the Memories chart? 
- How might those patterns help explain your bot’s driving?
- Would more training examples always make the bot better? 
- Why might the quality and variety of examples matter?
- If your bot keeps making the same mistake, what new driving examples would you collect?

### AI Generalization
- Predict how your bot will perform on a track it has not seen before. 
- What features of the new track might make driving easier or harder?
- If your bot succeeds on its training track but struggles on a different track, what does that tell you about what it learned?
- What variety of driving experiences might help the bot handle new tracks?
